# Manufacturer Content — Research Draft

This covers the ~30 manufacturers with the most toys in the collection. It's a
preview of the content/format only — nothing has been published to the site yet.

Two entries (**Osul**, **JATO & PEPE**) already had reliable history in your
own spreadsheet (in Portuguese) — I've translated those rather than
re-researching them externally.

For everyone else, content below was gathered via web search just now.
**Reliability notes:**
- Every fact below traces to at least one source link listed under it.
- Where sources disagreed or I couldn't confirm something, I said so rather
  than guessing — a few entries are intentionally thin.
- **Logos are the weak point of this pass**: almost none could be confirmed
  as a real, fetchable image file (search results pointed at Wikimedia Commons
  pages, but direct fetches were blocked, so I could not visually verify what's
  actually on them). I've marked each as "not found" or "unconfirmed — needs
  manual check" rather than risk linking the wrong image. I'd suggest manually
  checking the handful marked "unconfirmed" before using them, and we treat
  finding/verifying real logos as a separate follow-up pass.

## Osul
- **Manufacturer**: Luso-Celuloide de Henriques & Irmão, Lda
- **Country**: Portugal
- **Years active**: 1931–1989
- **Intro**: Founded by brothers Manuel (1886–1954) and Artur Henriques
  (1892–1965) in Espinho, Portugal. In the 1950s one of the brothers left to
  start a rival toy company, "Hércules," also in Espinho. Around that time
  Luso-Celuloide adopted the name "Osul" (a reversal of "Luso") and continued
  manufacturing plastic toys.
- **Logo**: not found (no image, only the text mark "Osul")
- **Sources**: family/company history recorded in the collection's own
  spreadsheet (original source not independently re-verified online)

## Lehmann
- **Manufacturer**: Ernst Paul Lehmann Patentwerk
- **Country**: Germany
- **Years active**: 1881 – present (nationalized in East Germany after WWII;
  family re-established the company in West Germany in 1948; acquired by
  Märklin in 2007, brand still distributed today)
- **Intro**: A German maker of tinplate wind-up toys founded in Brandenburg
  an der Havel, famous for intricate clockwork novelty toys such as the
  "Lehmann-Gnom," later expanding into vehicles, trains and airplanes.
  Regarded as one of the finest classic German tin toy makers.
- **Logo**: not found
- **Sources**: https://www.carters.com.au/index.cfm/index/4455-lehmann-company-germany-toys/, https://lehmanntoycollection.com/, https://kallistoys.com/toy-wiki/lehmann/

## Nomura
- **Manufacturer**: Nomura Toy Industrial Co. Ltd
- **Country**: Japan
- **Years active**: founded 1930s by Eijiro Nomura; tinplate toy production
  ran roughly late 1940s–1970s
- **Intro**: Tokyo-based maker (trademark "TN" in a diamond), one of the
  largest postwar Japanese tinplate toy producers — known for battery-operated
  robots, vehicles, and novelty toys widely exported through the 1950s–70s.
- **Logo**: not found
- **Sources**: https://www.fabtintoys.com/nomura/, https://www.ourstarblazers.com/vault/206/

- **Intro**: "Modern Toys" (mark "M-T") was a tinplate toy line of Masudaya,

## Marx
- **Manufacturer**: Louis Marx and Company
- **Country**: USA
- **Years active**: 1919–1980 (sold to Quaker Oats 1972; sold to
  Dunbee-Combex-Marx 1976; last US plant closed 1980)
- **Intro**: Founded in New York by Louis Marx and his brother David, it
  became the world's largest toy manufacturer by the 1950s, producing tin
  and plastic toys, playsets, and mechanical vehicles.
- **Logo**: unconfirmed — file referenced at commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Marx-logo.jpg

## Blomer & Schuler
- **Country**: Germany
- **Years active**: founded 1919 – production stopped 1974
- **Intro**: A Nuremberg manufacturer that initially made only wind-up
  clockwork mechanisms for other toymakers, then from around 1930 produced
  its own lithographed tin toys — notably animal toys like a walking "Jumbo"
  elephant and a tail-swinging turkey. Added tin automobiles around 1950.

## Gescha
- **Manufacturer**: Gebrüder Schmid
- **Country**: Germany
- **Years active**: founded 1923 – brand discontinued 1973
- **Intro**: Founded in Nuremberg by brothers Max and Ludwig Schmid,
  initially supplying parts to other toymakers before launching its own tin
  wind-up toys in 1933, including the popular "Express Boy" (1937). Sold to

- **Logo**: not found

## Technofix
- **Manufacturer**: Gebrüder Einfalt GmbH
- **Country**: Germany
- **Years active**: founded 1922 (as Gebrüder Einfalt); renamed Technofix

- **Intro**: Founded in Nuremberg by brothers Georg and Johann Einfalt with
  partner Franz Heussinger. Early toys marked "G.E." or "G.E.N."; the
  "Technofix" trademark dates to around 1926, formally adopted as the company
  name around 1935. Known for elaborate lithographed tin track toys.

## JATO & PEPE
- **Country**: Portugal
- **Years active**: JATO founded 1955; successor brand PEPE from the 1970s
- **Intro**: Founded by José Augusto Júnior (a tinsmith who began making
  tinplate toys — like baby rattles and noisemakers — in 1921). In 1930 he
  opened a new factory and pioneered Portugal's first wind-up ribbon-cord toy.
  In 1946 the company moved to Ermesinde, becoming "A Industrial de
  Quinquilharias de Ermesinde," then in 1955 renamed itself "Fábrica de
  Artigos em Plástico JATO," adopting the trademark "JATO." This is when
  icons like the Mercedes taxi, the VW "bread van," and miniature stoves
  appeared. After 1977, management passed to José Augusto Júnior's children,
  who adopted the brand PEPE (or PE-PE), continuing the tradition to this
  day under the name Bruplast.
- **Logo**: not found
- **Sources**: collection's own spreadsheet, translated from Portuguese; an
  independent Portuguese-language source exists at
  conservasdeportugal.com/historia-dos-brinquedos-aml-jaj-jato-pepe-e-m-m-s/
  but no English-language source was found.
